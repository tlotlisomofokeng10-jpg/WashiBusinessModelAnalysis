Business Model Analysis

Exploring the four primary business strategies for the Washi ecosystem.

💡

Base Assumptions

Calculations assume an average car wash price of R150. Peach Payments costs are estimated at ~2.95% + R1.50 per transaction. Firebase costs are estimated at ~R1.00 per active user/month (free until massive scale).

01

The "Free Washes" Model

The Original Concept

The user pays a high monthly subscription fee and gets a set number of "free" car washes per month.

Customer PriceR349 / mofor 4 washes
Payment Cost~R11.80per sub charge
Server Cost~R1.00per month
Partner Liability: Washi pays the car wash ~R80 - R100 per wash. If the user uses all 4 washes, Washi pays R320 - R400 to partners.

Advantages

  • High guaranteed upfront revenue (R349 per user).
  • Great for B2B fleet managers who want predictable monthly expenses.
  • "Breakage" is highly profitable if users wash less than allocated.

Disadvantages

⚠️ High financial risk
If users actually use all 4 washes, Washi will likely take a loss on that customer.
  • Very high barrier to entry for regular consumers. R349 is a lot to commit to upfront.
  • Hard to negotiate with car washes as Washi forces them to accept a severely discounted rate.
02

The "Upfront Discount" Model

The user pays a medium subscription fee to unlock a massive flat discount (e.g., 40% off) at any partner car wash.

Customer PriceR149 / mo
Payment Cost~R5.90per sub charge
Server Cost~R1.00per month
Partner Liability: The user pays the car wash directly, but at a 40% discount (R90 instead of R150).

Advantages

  • Immediate gratification for the user (savings seen instantly).
  • Zero financial liability for Washi per wash. The R149 subscription is almost pure profit.

Disadvantages

🛑 Extremely difficult to sell
Car washes hate giving massive upfront discounts because it destroys daily cash flow.
  • You will struggle to convince premium car washes to accept 40% less money upfront just to be on the app.
03

Micro-Sub + Cashback

Current Prototype

The user pays a small monthly fee to earn 15% cashback into an in-app wallet every wash. Full price is paid at the till.

Customer PriceR69 / mo
Payment Cost~R3.53per sub charge
Server Cost~R1.00per month
Partner Liability: Washi funds 5% and the Car Wash funds 10%. (Washi pays R7.50, Car wash pays R15.00 into the wallet).

Advantages

💡 Recommended
The most balanced and scalable model for South Africa.
  • Extremely easy to sell to users (R69 is the price of a coffee; it's a "no-brainer").
  • Easy to sell to car washes: They get full price at the till, and only fund a deferred 10% loyalty rebate.
  • Massive psychological "Lock-In": Users won't go to competitors with R100 in their Washi wallet.

Disadvantages

  • "Double Friction": User pays a sub *and* full price initially, marketing must explain wallet benefits clearly.
  • Must build and maintain a secure wallet/ledger database in Firebase to track digital money perfectly.
04

"Freemium" Wallet Model

Starbucks Strategy

App and wallet are 100% free. Earn 5% cashback. Upgrade to "Washi Plus" for a monthly fee to earn 20% and VIP perks.

Customer PriceFree / R99Base / Plus
Payment CostR0 / ~R4.42Base / Plus
Server Cost~R1.00per month
Partner Liability: Washi and the Car Wash split the cashback funding.

Advantages

🔥 Top Strategy
The absolute best strategy for rapid user growth.
  • Zero barrier to entry. Anyone can download and start earning.
  • Thousands of users can be onboarded quickly, giving leverage when negotiating with partners.
  • Highly effective upsell funnel (showing users potential earnings with Washi Plus).

Disadvantages

  • Slower path to initial revenue; supporting free users on servers before they upgrade.
  • Requires strict negotiations with car washes to fund cashback for free users (no sub revenue to subsidize).
